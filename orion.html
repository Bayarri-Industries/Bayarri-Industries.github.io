Active Development

Orion isn't a product. It's the spine. Every system Bayarri builds runs on it — Sentinel, Specter, everything that comes after. Pull Orion and the whole ecosystem stops. That's by design.

Most AI you'll encounter is a wrapper. Someone else's model, someone else's API, dressed up in a new interface and called innovation. Orion is none of that. Every weight, every layer, every architectural decision — built by one person, from scratch, with a precise vision for what defense AI actually needs to be.

What It Is

Orion is a 235 million parameter transformer — custom architecture, custom tokenizer, custom training pipeline. No pretrained weights borrowed from anyone. The same architectural components used in today's frontier models — RMSNorm, SwiGLU feed-forward networks, 32,768-token BPE vocabulary — applied exclusively to Bayarri's 9 intelligence domains. Not the whole internet. The right knowledge, trained right.

On top of that sits the Consciousness Loop — an autonomous cycle that runs in the background whether you're watching or not. Orion identifies gaps in its own knowledge, queues them for research, pulls from credible sources, and updates itself. It doesn't wait to be prompted. It doesn't ask for permission. It learns.

Why It Matters

One AI. Gets smarter over time. Understands the battlefield. Powers everything else. When Sentinel flags a threat, Orion classifies it. When Specter picks up a contact, Orion tells you what it is and where it's going. Orion is what separates a collection of tools from an actual system.

Where It's Going

Orion V2 is actively training across all 9 intelligence domains simultaneously — math, coding, systems, AI architecture, electronics, physics, quantum, aerodynamics, and security. An autonomous research pipeline feeds it continuously: when Orion encounters something it doesn't know, it queues the topic at high priority, crawls credible sources, and updates its own knowledge base without any human intervention. The target is 90%+ benchmark parity with commercial models on domain-specific tasks — then surpassing them where it counts: specialized domains, battlefield inference, and scenarios no public dataset was ever trained on. The flywheel only spins one direction.
